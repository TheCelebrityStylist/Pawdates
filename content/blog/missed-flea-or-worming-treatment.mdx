Missing a dose is the most ordinary thing in the world. The date slid
past on a busy week, or the new tube is still in its Amazon box. The
important thing is that flea and worming products behave very
differently when you fall behind — and the right recovery depends on
which one you missed.

## First: which kind of gap is this?

**Worming is a "point-in-time" treatment.** A wormer kills the worms
present in the gut on the day you give it and then does nothing more —
it leaves no protective residue. So a late worming dose does not leave
your pet suddenly exposed; it means there was a longer stretch in which
an untreated infection could have matured and shed eggs into your home
and garden. The fix is simply to dose now and reset the calendar.

**Flea treatment is "residual" protection.** A spot-on or tablet keeps
working for a set window — usually a month, sometimes three. Miss the
next dose and protection does not stop dead on the due date; it fades
across the final days of the window. A week over, cover is reduced. Two
to three weeks over is where an infestation quietly seeds itself.

## The rule that matters most: never double up

Whatever you missed, give **one** normal dose and continue from there.
Doubling is not a shortcut and can be actively unsafe — most of all
across species. A dog flea product containing permethrin can kill a cat,
and cats are far more sensitive to many parasiticides than dogs are. In
a [multi-pet household this is where the real danger lives](/blog/multi-pet-household-medications):
grabbing the wrong tube for the wrong animal.

## Recovering a worming schedule

1. Give the appropriate wormer for your pet's current weight today.
2. Restart the interval from today's date — not from the old missed date.
3. If it has been months, or your pet hunts, scavenges or lives with
   young children, ask your vet whether a follow-up dose two to four
   weeks later is worth it, since some worms have a pre-patent period
   during which a single dose can miss a fresh batch.

The European guidance body ESCCAP recommends that a deworming programme
be built around risk rather than habit — most pets need treating at
least four times a year, and higher-risk animals monthly. Their
[Worm Control in Dogs and Cats guideline](https://www.esccap.org/guidelines/)
sets out the risk groups if you want to place your own pet.

## Recovering a flea schedule

1. Apply or give a single dose now.
2. Reset the monthly (or three-monthly) reminder from today.
3. If you have seen live fleas, treat **every** pet in the home on the
   same cycle and wash bedding at 60°C — the environment holds the bulk
   of the population, and ESCCAP's
   [ectoparasite control guideline](https://www.esccap.org/guidelines/)
   stresses that treating one animal in isolation rarely works.
4. Keep going for at least three months. Pupae are cocooned and
   insecticide-resistant, and hatch in waves for weeks after you think
   you have won.
